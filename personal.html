Personal

Please be aware that this page contains a degree of personal disclosure that may be unexpected in a professional context, as well as discussions of potentially distressing experiences.
Some history about myself: I grew up in the suburbs of Beirut (Lebanon), in a pretty poor family and neighbourhood. My parents are uneducated (neither of them completed middle school studies). My mother is a homemaker who worked briefly as a cleaner, and my father was a carpenter who died more than 10 years ago. I have an older and a younger brother in a similar age bracket. My family was pretty dysfunctional, in the sense that daily physical and verbal abuse was the norm. Many people assume that my family’s religious background is Islam, which is fair given that most of the Arab world is. But mine is from the Maronite Christianity faith. I don’t think it’s much better in terms of personal liberties, in the way it's practiced by many Lebanese people, including my parents. It was definitely tough growing up in such an environment, and my family didn’t really support my ambitions as a woman, even when I excelled at school. They thought for more or less okayish reasons (didn’t seem to me so at the time, the world is ugly), I would be better off quitting education, and marrying a more well-off man.
Regarding my value system: I highly favor center-left/social democratic ideology, I’m a feminist, a minimalist, an atheist, I care about the environment, I think people should be nice to one another or at least try to. I also think that difference in opinion should be tolerated and that diversity is a strength.
Regarding my personality traits: I’m introverted, I’m pretty reflective, I’m generally calm, I don’t enjoy small talk, I like having few significant people in my life rather than many surface-level ones.
Fields that have so far always captured my interest are computer science, linguistics, natural language processing, history, philosophy and art. I already dug well into each of them I would say, but it’s a never ending intellectual endeavour. However, I’m also generally curious about many things, and I also strive to have a well-rounded understanding of the world, so I also possess general knowledge in finance, international politics, economy, sociology, and biology for example.
Things I enjoy in random order: cooking, hiking, running, reading (mostly non-fiction: I read some BBC articles on a daily basis, books, and of course academic literature - academic papers; books; and other written material!), watching movies/series (mostly comedies and documentaries/docuseries), listening to music (mainly Pop and R&B), exploring other cultures (through different means, such as intentional travel; reading; documentaries; visiting museums; tasting/making different foods; meeting/engaging with people from such countries), talking to people (including people from different countries or views), discussing different topics (I tend to be abstract in my discussions but I’m also fine with the concrete), daydreaming, being alone, cleaning and organizing things (because you gotta at least put up with the mundane!), being intimate with someone I fancy.
Other random things about me in random order: I’m bisexual (more like pansexual), my biological sex is female and I identify as one (I’m also ok with they/them pronouns). I don’t know how to drive a car, nor a motorcycle, nor how to ride a bicycle. I eat healthy mostly. I don’t smoke cigarettes, nor do drugs, nor drink alcohol (I've never been into any of that, but I drank alcohol socially in my early 20s). I never watch sports on television. I don’t play videogames. I don’t know how to swim nor ski. I’ve been diagnosed with depression, anxiety, obsessive compulsive disorder, and bipolar disorder for many years (not all at once, but depression always), and I’ve been taking medication for many years (and of course doing therapy, at different times). I have never been married and I don’t have children. I have been in multiple short-term relationships in my early 20s mainly. I haven’t been in a relationship for the past bunch of years even when I dated many people (most lasted either 1 or 2 dates). I prefer monogamy over open relationships even when I tried it in the past. I'm really good with money. I like coffee!.
Frequently Asked Questions (more like questions I came up with, because I can!):
Why are you sharing so much about yourself? → Because I meet a lot of humans at first online in this day and age, and I don't mind sharing such things about myself. I tend to say such things when asked anyway.
Do you ever go partying? → No, I felt too socially awkward and out of place when I did when I was younger. I was really trying to fit in, but I just felt too empty.
Do you ever put makeup on? or remove your glasses? → No, I don't own anything makeup related, and I would go blind without my glasses. I don't think it's inherently wrong to do such things, but I don't have that sort of energy myself. I never did.
Do you hate religious people? → No, I think each person should be respected for whatever religious view they hold, as long as they don't impose it on others. I just was never able to believe in a supreme being as described in religious texts I know of, and I'd rather base my belief system on fields such as biology and history.
Have you ever been harassed sexually? → Yes, I have, multiple times, including in Europe. Thankfully, I have never been raped. I think many women (and other individuals), experience such thing in their lifetime, and a lot of work needs to be done to address this properly, in the sexist society we still live in.
Do you always talk in English? → I live and worked with an international community for some years now, so yes, I mostly talk in English on a day to day basis, and I am really comfortable with this. I also consume(d) a lot of English content. However, I talk with my family primarily in Lebanese Arabic (with occasional French and English code-switching which I have to translate again to Arabic most of the time😂), and I talk in basic broken German with Austrians/Austrian residents when necessary😅
Do you like Austria?! → yes, I mostly really do. Even though it took a while to get accustomed to some things (which I am not sure I could explain easily in writing!). The quality of life is pretty high for example, and some systems are pretty efficient (e.g., administrative, medical, etc.).
How do I pronounce your name? → Sandy is pronounced the way you pronounce Sandy in English, and my family name is really hard to pronounce the way it's pronounced in Modern Standard Arabic or Lebanese Arabic, so read it however you want. I prefer people to call me using my first name anyway. Specific people in my life have assigned me few nicknames, but most people call me Sandy. If we don't know each other much yet, I prefer if you don't assign me a nickname.
Aren't you afraid of people judging you very negatively due to sharing your mental health issues?! → Part of the reason this is shared is to raise some awareness around the topic. A fair percentage of humans will experience a mental illness in their lifetime, and most people hide it. There is so much stigma around the topic. More people should talk openly about it.
If you weren't an academic, what would you like to be? → I know it's a long shot, but I'd love to be a stand-up comedian! I have a lot of jokes running through my head, including some pretty dark, dry humor that's somewhat similar in style to George Carlin's. So, yes, if anyone at Netflix is reading this, please contact me!!!😋😂
How has your perception of academia changed over the years? → I've become pretty disillusioned with it. Given the environment I grew up in, I somewhat naively thought that being surrounded by highly educated people would also mean being surrounded by more enlightened, egalitarian, and humane individuals. My experiences with harassment and workplace bullying, notably by men from Europe, have challenged that assumption quite a bit. Being a woman from a disadvantaged socioeconomic background and a non-Western country has also made me particularly conscious of the power dynamics and social hierarchies that exist within academia. It's quite unsettling to witness people who intellectually understand concepts such as inequality, discrimination, and abuse of power, yet fail to reflect on their own behavior in that regard. I think one of the more painful realizations of my adult life has been that intellectual sophistication doesn't necessarily translate into moral sophistication. I still deeply enjoy intellectual pursuits and scholarly work, but I've learned to dissociate that from my perception of academia as a social institution. Perhaps social criticism disguised as stand-up comedy is my true calling after all!😋 (Netflix. Again. Help.😋)
Why haven't you spoken about these experiences before? → This is probably the first time in my life that I've been in a sufficiently stable financial position to be able to defend myself without being terrified of the potential consequences. When you come from a disadvantaged socioeconomic background, with no financial safety net or family to fall back on, you tend to be considerably more cautious about challenging people who have a high degree of power over your professional trajectory. It's quite difficult to stand up for yourself when doing so could jeopardize your livelihood. I think people who have always had some form of socioeconomic security tend to underestimate the extent to which financial vulnerability can deprive an individual of their ability to exercise their rights in practice. My previous silence shouldn't be mistaken for acceptance of the way I was treated. I simply couldn't afford the potential consequences of speaking up. It is very sad that, despite universities having worked towards greater diversity and gender equality for much of my lifetime, they still seem to be failing miserably in many respects, with men continuing to disproportionately occupy positions of power and benefit from the very structures these institutions claim to be trying to change.
Do you think you'll find your way back to academia, or is this the end? → I’ve found myself quite lost professionally on several occasions in the past, only to unexpectedly find my way back to academia. My academic trajectory has certainly been far from linear. However, I wouldn’t necessarily count on things unfolding that way again. There’s a fairly high probability that I won’t be professionally involved in academia anymore, and I’m trying to make peace with that possibility. As much as I enjoy scholarly work, I’ve come to realize that having a genuine passion for intellectual pursuits and being able to build a sustainable career in academia are two quite different things. I suppose I’ll continue to pursue my intellectual interests in one capacity or another, regardless of where I end up professionally.
Why do you feel compelled to voice these views publicly rather than simply move on? → I generally don’t feel the need to impose my worldview on other individuals, and I’m quite comfortable with people holding views that differ considerably from mine. However, I do think there’s a distinction between imposing one’s views on others and insisting that one’s perspective be afforded the space to exist and be heard. As someone whose background and personal circumstances place me at the intersection of several underrepresented groups within academia, I feel somewhat of a moral responsibility to articulate perspectives that, in my experience, are virtually absent from the very institutional environments that claim to value diversity and inclusion. I find it problematic that institutions can advocate for diversity while being considerably less receptive to the perspectives and lived experiences that such diversity actually brings with it. Meaningful inclusion, in my view, entails more than simply allowing people from diverse backgrounds to occupy academic spaces; it also entails ensuring that they can pursue their scholarly work without being subjected to harassment, intimidation, or workplace bullying, and that they can question the structures and practices of those spaces without jeopardizing their professional existence. I don’t expect people to agree with my views, nor do I expect any form of preferential treatment. I do, however, think I should be able to exist within such environments, express my views, have my personal and professional boundaries respected, and be afforded the same degree of dignity and professional legitimacy as individuals whose backgrounds and perspectives are more closely aligned with those of the people occupying positions of power. I don’t think any of these constitute particularly unreasonable expectations.
